A tailorable workout builder, a macro tracker and a calorie-burn calculator. No account, no subscription, no feature that quietly moves behind a paywall next year. Everything you type stays in this browser. Better software, built by AI — because you deserved it yesterday.

Not medical advice. These are general-purpose calculators, not a diagnosis, a prescription or a treatment plan. Calorie and macro figures are estimates from published reference tables and will differ from your body. Talk to a doctor or a qualified professional before starting a new exercise or eating plan, especially if you are pregnant, injured, or managing a medical condition.

01Workout builder

Pick what you have and what you want to train. Every line is editable — change the sets and reps, swap an exercise you dislike, or drop it entirely.

Rest between sets is the number under the goal you picked, not a rule. Free-weight and machine loads are yours to choose — we do not know what you can lift, and an app that guesses a barbell weight for a stranger is how people get hurt.

Work out a daily calorie goal (optional)

Estimated with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the one dietitians use. It needs a few more numbers about you, and none of them leave this browser.

Pick the activity level for your life minus the workouts you log in this tracker — those are already counted as burn above. Choosing “very active” and logging your run counts that run twice, which is the most common way one of these numbers ends up several hundred calories too high.

MET burn is gross expenditure: it includes the calories you would have burned sitting still for those minutes, which is why it reads higher than a treadmill’s “net” number. It is an average for an average body, and it does not know your fitness, your terrain or your heart rate. The calorie goal above is an estimate from a published equation, not a prescription: it is right to within about 10% for roughly 7 people in 10, and it cannot see your medical history. Treat it as a starting number to adjust against what the scale actually does over a few weeks, and take any real decision to a professional.
