Italian Hall — seventy-three dead, and no fire

Calumet, Michigan, 24 December 1913

Someone shouted 'Fire!' at a strikers' Christmas party in 1913. There was no fire. Seventy-three people died on the stairs.

In the fifth month of a bitter copper strike, the miners' union women's auxiliary held a Christmas Eve party for strikers' families on the second floor of the Italian Hall. Someone shouted "Fire!" There was no fire. Seventy-three people were crushed to death on the stairway down — most of them children.

What was never established

Disputed — the coroner's inquest ran for days and named no cause. The man who shouted was never identified. Witnesses said he wore the button of the anti-union Citizens' Alliance; that was never proven and is argued about still, as is the claim that the doors were held shut from the street. A Congressional subcommittee took testimony in 1914 and the question is open.

What is left

The hall was demolished in 1984. Its sandstone arch stands alone in a memorial park, and you can walk through it.
