HMT Lancastria — censored the same afternoon

Off Saint-Nazaire, 17 June 1940

Britain's worst maritime disaster happened two weeks after Dunkirk, and Churchill suppressed it before the survivors were dry.

Two weeks after Dunkirk, in the evacuation nobody remembers, the liner Lancastria lay off Saint-Nazaire taking on troops, RAF personnel and civilians. Her peacetime capacity was 2,200; accounts of how many were aboard run from four to nine thousand. A Junkers Ju 88 hit her at 3:48. She sank in about twenty minutes. 2,477 were pulled from the water.

How many died

Disputed — the death toll was never officially established. Estimates run from roughly 1,700 to well over 4,000, and on the commonly cited figures it is the worst loss of life in British maritime history — exceeding the Titanic and the Lusitania together. Churchill suppressed the story that day and survivors were told not to speak of it. The news broke only on 26 July.

One correction

Debunked — you will read everywhere that the file is sealed under a hundred-year notice until 2040. The National Archives holds and releases Lancastria material now, and no such embargo has ever been produced by anyone repeating the claim. The censorship is documented. The 2040 story is not.

Part of this story is labelled DEBUNKED and DISPUTED above, in the paragraph it belongs to. We label rather than cut: a claim the record does not carry is still part of how the story was told, and you are entitled to know which part is which.
