Not a book of quotes. A ledger of durations.

Quotes tell you how somebody felt. A count tells you what they survived. “Never give up” is a sentiment; “133 days on an eight-foot raft” is a measurement you can put your own number next to. And a count is falsifiable — every one here is checked against a named source, so if a number is wrong you can prove it. Each record is written the same four ways:

The countHow long it lasted, as precisely as the record allows.
What happenedThe situation, plainly stated. No dramatisation.
What they actually didThe operational detail — the wire bent into a hook, the pocket knife. The part worth carrying.
The sourceWhere you can go and check it yourself.

A sample record

Poon Lim — 133 days

What happened. The merchant ship Benlomond was torpedoed in the South Atlantic and sank in about two minutes. Poon Lim, a ship's steward, was the only survivor. He found an eight-foot square wooden raft and got onto it.

What he actually did. When the tinned rations ran out he collected rainwater in the canvas of his life jacket, pulled the wire out of his flashlight, bent it into a hook and fished. He caught seabirds. Ships passed and did not stop. He kept the raft's routine going anyway, and after 133 days Brazilian fishermen found him nine miles off the Pará coast — he walked ashore under his own power.

Source: Wikipedia, “Poon Lim”; War History Online. One of twelve. The other eleven — and the blank ledger — are in the file.

Then start your own count

At the back is a blank ledger. One line a day: the date, the number of days you are into this thing, and one sentence about what you did — not what you felt. Print as many copies as you want; that is the whole point of buying a file instead of renting an app. A missed day does not reset the count.

The brand rule, in plain sight. Twelve verified records, not two hundred padded ones. Every count is read against a named source printed under it. Where reputable sources disagree — Ederle's crossing is cited as 14:31 and, shore-to-shore, 14:34 — the disagreement is printed, not quietly resolved. No invented dialogue, no unsourced quotations. Carries a plain non-therapy note and a crisis-line pointer, because “endure it” is not the right answer to every situation.
