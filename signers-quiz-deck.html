America 250: The Signers Quiz Deck

Be the one at the cookout who knows the real story — and knows which famous ones aren't true.

Fifty-six men signed the Declaration of Independence. Most Americans can name three. This deck turns the other fifty-three into something you can actually play — forty question-and-answer cards, grouped into five themed suits, that work at a table, in a car, or at the front of a classroom.

The rule that makes it different: when a beloved story is only a legend, the deck says so. King George's spectacles. The Liberty Bell ringing on July 4. The deck names them, explains where the story came from, and gives you the record instead — so you never repeat a tall tale as fact.

Built for the people who want to be stumped

"Hard 4th of July Trivia Questions to Stump Your Friends" and "10 Surprising Facts About 4th of July History" are the same request from two directions: give me something I have not heard. Forty cards on the signers is where that material actually lives, because it is the part of the story that got compressed into a single sentence in school.

It gives well

People asking "What are the best 250th anniversary gifts to give?" are usually looking at mugs. A deck of forty questions is the gift that gets used more than once — print it, sleeve it, hand it to the history reader in the family, and it comes back out every Fourth.

How to play

  • Table play — print, cut, deal one suit per round
  • Car play — read from the master list, no printing, no pieces to lose

  • Two-minute version — pull five cards at random and see who survives
